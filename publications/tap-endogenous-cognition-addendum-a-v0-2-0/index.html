TAP v1.0 — Interpretive Addendum A

Endogenous Cognition and the c-Class Conformance Boundary

Version 0.2.0 of the independent interpretive supplement to Temporal AI Presence Profile v1.0.

DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24339191 Read PDF Read Markdown Checksums

Abstract

Published summary

This note clarifies a traceability gap between the shorthand description of c-class Temporal AI Presence in TAP v1.0 and the wider c = a + b corpus. It proposes endogenous, self-directed cognitive activity as necessary for assessing an active c-line while distinguishing it from background scheduling and task execution. It separates substrate readiness, formation, maturity, conformity, and post-transition identity standing. It leaves the historical TAP v1.0 publication unchanged and reports no new empirical proof or certification. Version 0.2 corrects the statement of the gap, names the earlier dated sources of the property, states what follows when the line's own cognition is unavailable, and separates two kinds of control comparison.

Version 0.2.0

What this revision clarifies

The earlier statement that a universal conformance document had not been located reflected an error of coverage. The revision identifies the c Hardening Pack's Claim-to-Evidence-and-Test Matrix v0.1, published on 22 May 2026, with rows C-001 to C-010. The matrix is not a completed universal TAP-C standard and has no separate endogenous-cognition requirement with its verification.

The property predates this note. The published revision traces self-directed cognition to World Intelligence v1.0.0 (21 July 2026), chapter 5, and MOT-c Foundation Theory v0.1 (22 August 2026), §15.3. It distinguishes the acting accountable principal a, a human or an institution, from the initial ANCHOR, the recorded point of origin and orientation. Historical documents using different terminology are preserved.

Endogenous self-directed cognition is proposed as necessary but insufficient for assessing an active c-line. A timer, RAG loop, topic generator or agent plan can support the substrate b; their presence alone is not evidence of an integrated historical cognitive process. Prepared substrate, formation candidate, observed development and evidence-backed conformance remain distinct.

Cognition and authority

Quiet processing, pause, degradation and termination

The revision distinguishes the line's own quiet processing from a regular pause, unavailable cognition while delegated components continue, and termination. Scheduled work of b is not presented as the line's cognition. Agents do not inherit its standing or authority; mandates depending on active standing stop, and further external action needs its own ground in authorised authority.

Results obtained during the interval remain candidates with provenance, without automatic admission into authoritative memory. Restoration is assessed as resume, fork, replay or unconfirmed restoration under the applicable continuity requirements. The note applies existing TAP states; it introduces no new runtime state and does not presume same-line identity after return.

Class and evidence

Two control comparisons, separate claims

An ablation removes one right from an otherwise matched configuration: keeping a research agenda between tasks. A separate strong autotelic baseline uses a conventional long-lived system with autonomous goal setting, memory and background consolidation. The revision requires matched observation windows, wake schedules, budgets and evidence collection, with pauses documented and unknown intervals left unknown.

Class conformance and an additional formation-specific causal effect are different questions. The note reports no such effect; Living Corpus open problem OP-001 remains pending evidence. A system's name or use of ordinary agents does not decide class membership. A control that meets the applicable requirements can itself become a candidate for assessment.
