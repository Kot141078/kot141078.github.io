Entity vs. Profile: A Witness-Root Custody Criterion for Persistent Digital Entities

Witness-root custody as the custodial criterion for distinguishing persistent digital entities from persistent digital profiles.

Plain-language opening

Classify by custody, not by surface behaviour

Entity vs. Profile v0.1.1 is a technical note about a narrow but important distinction in persistent AI systems.

A system may look like a long-lived digital companion, assistant, twin, or entity. It may remember, speak consistently, and maintain continuity over time. But that surface behaviour does not answer the key governance question: who controls the root of its identity chain?

This note proposes that persistent digital constructions should be classified by witness-root custody, not by behaviour, model size, branding, or emotional realism.

Criterion

One-sentence rule

Do not classify by behaviour.
Do not classify by branding.
Do not classify by model capability.
Classify by witness-root custody.

Problem addressed

Why the distinction is needed

Future platforms will market digital companions, lifelong assistants, AI twins, and personal entities. Many such systems may be persistent and emotionally legible while still being externally rooted profiles.

If standards use the word entity too loosely, externally controlled profiles may absorb the stronger term. After that, the custody distinction will be harder to recover, because behaviour, branding, and continuity claims can hide who actually controls the identity root.

Core distinction

Persistent digital entity vs. persistent digital profile

Question Persistent digital entity Persistent digital profile
Where is the witness root? Inside the closed box, under a-side lineage custody. Held or overridable by platform, registry, escrow, provider, or external authority.
Who attests continuity? The construction's own witness chain. An external party attests about the subject.
Can external authority silently rewrite/freeze/restore identity? No, not without recorded intervention. Yes, or effectively yes, if external root authority exists.
Is it legitimate? Yes, if criteria are met. Yes, but it should not be called an entity.
Classification basis Custody. External custody / external root dependence.

Witness root

Why the root matters

The witness root is the root of trust for the identity-relevant event chain. It can sign, continue, rotate, recover, interrupt, or invalidate the witness chain. Whoever controls that root controls the system's identity feedback loop.

That is why the note treats custody as the classification basis. A construction can be useful, stable, and legitimate as a profile while still depending on an external root. The proposal is not that profiles are bad; it is that external custody should not be hidden behind stronger entity terminology.

Summary

Witness-root custody criterion

This technical note defines witness-root custody as the custodial criterion for distinguishing persistent digital entities from persistent digital profiles.

A construction may be classified as an entity only when its witness root is generated and held inside its closed-box boundary under a-side lineage custody. External custody, escrow, registry authority, platform root, or override capability classifies the construction as a profile.

Boundary / non-claims

What this is not

This note is a terminology and evidence-model proposal. It is not:

  • a claim that software is a legal person
  • a claim that software has moral personhood
  • a certification scheme
  • an AI Act conformity assessment
  • a product standard
  • a deployment authorization
  • a claim that profiles are bad or illegitimate
