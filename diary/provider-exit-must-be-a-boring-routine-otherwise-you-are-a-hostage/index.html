Provider exit must be a boring routine. Otherwise, you are a hostage.

2026-09-17

An argument for provider exit, persistent local state, replaceable models, and resilient local-first AI infrastructure.

AI Infrastructure AI LocalAI ArtificialIntelligence OpenWeights Future of Work

Provider exit must be a boring routine. Otherwise, you are a hostage.

For years, people have been preaching about local AI, hiding behind lofty buzzwords like privacy, sovereignty, and control.

All of that is great, but there is a far more brutal argument: your work shouldn’t stop because someone else pulled the plug.

If your entire intellectual pipeline hangs on one cloud model, one subscription, one corporate provider, and its quota system,- you own nothing. You have no production capacity. You are simply renting someone else's brain.

Their model might be genius. Their company might be incredible. You might be standing there with your hand out, begging them to take your money for a Pro tier.

And then a morning like today arrives, and they just cut off your oxygen. Quotas burn to the ground, sales are halted, and you sink right along with the rest of the panicking crowd.

This fundamentally changes the hard rules of AI architecture.

A resilient system is not a specific model. The model is a consumable. A replaceable compute node. A swappable cartridge.

Today you drain compute from OpenAI.

Tomorrow you switch to Anthropic. The day after tomorrow, you spin up a heavy open-weight model on the hardware humming under your own desk.

The bottom line is simple: a provider pulling the plug must not destroy your working ecosystem.

Memory remains. History remains. Architecture, relationships, context,- everything stays in place. The pipeline keeps moving. Yes, overall throughput might temporarily drop. Complex tasks might have to wait for an external supercomputer.

But your system has no right to die just because someone in Silicon Valley reset your counter to zero.

Hence the only viable engineering criterion: Provider exit must be boring.

Kill one model. Plug in another. Keep working.

This is exactly why local AI hardware is no longer a toy for geeks and paranoids.

It is your core asset. Your heavy machinery. A local model might be dumber, slower, and not as shiny as a cloud flagship. But it grinds 24/7. It doesn’t demand money for every token, it doesn’t choke during peak hours, and it holds the line when cloud giants collapse under the load.

The cloud needs to become what it actually is: a steroid. Expensive, on-demand intelligence.

But under no circumstances is it the place where your entire working existence should live and depend.

Today, one corporation has the best model on the market. Tomorrow, another will take its place. I have seen enough tech monopolies rise and fall to know that they come and go.

But your work must not stop.
