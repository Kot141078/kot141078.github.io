Saturday traffic report from the AI highway.

2026-08-29

A humorous AI-industry traffic report contrasting model capability, infrastructure, embodiment, interfaces, and continuity.

Saturday traffic report from the AI highway.

Elon has strapped a small robot workforce to a rocket-truck and is trying to make intelligence touch the physical world at maximum speed.

Jensen is driving a car that appears to be only partly rendered, yet somehow already supplies the road beneath most of the traffic.

Mark has turned the windshield into a pair of smart glasses and seems to be sharing the journey with several billion invisible passengers.

OpenAI and Anthropic are not racing in the same class. They arrive as tankers, carrying general-purpose cognitive feedstock and alignment distillate. Less theatrical, perhaps, but much of the traffic would not travel far without the fuel.

Then there is the small station at the side of the road.

It does not manufacture chips, train frontier models or sell hypercars. It works on the awkward conversion problem:

How does capability become a persistent presence?
When does stored memory become continuity rather than an archive?
Who is authorized to act?
What survives a model replacement, a hardware change, a memory edit or the loss of the human anchor?
And when someone restores a copy, who exactly has returned?

That is why the station carries the less glamorous signs: c = a + b, ANCHOR, L4, PASC, SHA, DOI, witness records and ledgers.

The pump price is infinity, because continuity is apparently not sold by the gallon.

A model is a tool, not the house.
Access is not identity.
Replay is not return.
A memorial is not resurrection.

These sound like philosophical distinctions until disks fail, processes restart, logs disagree and somebody asks the system to do something irreversible. Then philosophy becomes architecture: provenance, custody, bounded authority, durable logs, rollback, witnesses and explicit limits.

On the ground, the work is less cinematic. Hardware racks hum. Hashes are checked. Journals are reconciled. The kettle continues to ignore the roadmap.

The road needs all of these layers: embodiment, compute, interface, model capability and continuity.

Most of the industry is asking how fast the machine can travel and how far it can scale.

I am asking who, or what, is still travelling after the engine, chassis and driver have all changed.

Not a better car.

A different discipline.

The cat is responsible for audit and has declined to comment.
