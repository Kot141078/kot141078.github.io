AGI is becoming a strangely unstable term.

2026-10-04

A reflection on changing AI terminology and Advanced Global Intelligence as coordination across different participants and capabilities.

Advanced Global Intelligence AI ArtificialIntelligence Superintelligence Future of AI AIResearch

AGI is becoming a strangely unstable term.

For years, it meant Artificial General Intelligence: the moment when one AI system would become sufficiently general to cross an imagined threshold.

But the threshold keeps moving.

Models can already write code, research, reason across domains, use tools, coordinate agents and operate for increasingly long periods. Yet every new capability produces the same answer:

“Not AGI yet.”

And now we have another linguistic temptation: rename AI into SI — Super Intelligence.

I don’t think that helps.

Artificial Intelligence is a useful umbrella term precisely because it does not pretend that every model, agent or application is already superintelligent.

If everything becomes “SI,” the term loses information.

A chatbot is SI. A coding model is SI. An autonomous agent is SI. A future system vastly beyond human capability is also SI.

Then what exactly did we describe?

Technology does not become more advanced because we renamed it.

Perhaps the more interesting question is not whether AI should become SI, but whether we have been looking at AGI from the wrong scale.

I have long used another expansion of the same acronym:

Advanced Global Intelligence.

Not one giant model. Not a machine that suddenly becomes “general.” Not a replacement for humanity.

A larger intelligence formed through connected humans, AI systems, agents, tools, institutions, local machines and persistent digital participants — each contributing different capabilities.

In that picture, intelligence does not need to become universal inside one box.

It becomes global through coordination.

That distinction matters.

A model can become more capable without becoming the whole system. An agent can act without becoming its owner. Memory can persist without becoming authority. And many different intelligences can participate in something larger than any one of them.

Maybe AGI will not arrive as a dramatic moment when someone announces:

“We crossed the line.”

Maybe one day we will simply notice that the network around us has become an Advanced Global Intelligence.

And that we have been building it for years.
