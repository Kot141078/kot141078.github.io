"One Ribbon Is Easy to Cut\n\nImagine the opening of a new national AI data centre. Government officials cut a red ribbon. Cameras flash. The speeches promise technological sovereignty.\n\nAcross town, the owner of a small engineering firm tries to cut something less ceremonial: a contract with its AI provider.\n\nBut years of drawings, decisions, maintenance history and corrections are tied to that provider's systems. Can it export usable history? Switch models? Keep essential work running with a local fallback?\n\nIn my World Intelligence research, the distinction is deliberate: models are replaceable sources of capability. The long-term history, permissions and obligations of a person or accountable institution are a different layer.\n\nThe test of sovereign AI is not whether a government can cut a ribbon in front of its own data centre.\n\nIt is whether its citizens can cut a contract without cutting away years of their own work."

"AI Improved the Output. Did It Train the Person?\n\nImagine a company giving everyone an AI assistant. The reports improve.
