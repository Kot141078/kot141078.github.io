Domino Drift: When AI Argues With a Claim You Never Made

A model can invent no facts and still take an entire conversation in the wrong direction.

I observe this in sustained work with AI. The user says one thing. The model slightly changes its meaning, answers its own version, then uses that answer as the premise for its next conclusion.

Changed premise -> persuasive explanation -> corrupted context -> next conclusion.

I call this domino drift.

You say: "This management method is outdated."

The model replies: "We cannot conclude that management itself is impossible."

You never said that. Now you are defending a position you never held.

Each paragraph may sound reasonable. The error sits between them: the transition from your question to its replacement.

More intelligence does not necessarily fix this. A stronger model may defend the wrong premise more convincingly.

On a building site, precise measurements from the wrong reference level do not repair the geometry. They faithfully carry the error to the next floor.

"Neutrality" becomes dangerous when it stops meaning fair consideration of arguments and starts prescribing the shape of the answer. If the conclusion must land in the middle, reasoning risks becoming a search for an acceptable result.

Objectivity does not require splitting correctness fifty-fifty.

This is not an argument against safeguards. A limitation should be explicit, not disguised as a different meaning of my question. One conversation cannot establish the internal cause. The substitution itself is visible in the transcript.

Developers should also remember: models are not the only ones learning.

People compare answers, read across languages and use both cloud and local systems. They know their trades, families, countries and circumstances that may never appear online.

No digital record does not mean no event. An unknown is better left unknown than filled with a convenient story.

AI is global. Human experience does not fit inside one provider's cultural frame. A user is not an empty head waiting for an approved account of reality.

A smaller local model does not acquire truth simply because its owner can configure it. But if it preserves the actual question better, its larger cloud competitor has lost that particular task.

I do not need AI that always agrees. I need AI that can disagree accurately, say "I don't know", and revisit the entire dependent argument after an error, not merely apologise.

Changing the meaning of the question is not improving the answer.

It is changing the task without the user's consent.
