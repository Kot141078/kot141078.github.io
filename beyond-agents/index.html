Beyond Agents

A dated route from agentic task execution to c as the continuity-bearing layer above replaceable models, agents, tools, and swarms.

A model computes. An agent executes. A c continues.

This page does not introduce another protocol. It records how one public question developed over time:

If agents are workers, what preserves identity, intent, memory, authority boundaries, and consequence when the workers change?

The answer proposed across this corpus is not a larger agent, a super-agent, or a swarm promoted into a subject. It is a different architectural layer: c.

Structural shift

What changed across the timeline

Output → trajectory

The central object stopped being the answer and became the sequence a system can carry without losing its identity or claim boundaries.

Swarm → governed center

More agents increase capability. They do not automatically create continuity, standing, responsibility, or a lawful center of action.

Memory → consequence

Stored text is not enough. Memory matters when it changes future behavior while preserving provenance, uncertainty, and the right to challenge.

Capability → bounded authority

A worker may be capable of acting without being authorized to own memory, promote experience, change identity, or release a result.

Demo → habitat

Persistent intelligence needs power, cooling, local memory, maintenance, budgets, shutdown paths, and a place where time is real.

Claim → evidence surface

Public language became progressively more constrained by witness, deterministic checks, migration tests, non-claims, and falsifiable boundaries.

Claim boundary

What this timeline does — and does not — establish

It establishes a dated public development route inside Ivan Kotov's authored corpus. It helps readers inspect when distinctions appeared, how they changed, and where they were later formalized.

It does not establish priority over every similar idea, independent validation, consciousness, personhood, entity status, production safety, deployment authorization, or that every persistent AI system is a c.

Diary posts and Vision provide authorial and developmental context. DOI-bound publications, tagged repositories, manifests, checksums, tests, and explicitly named evidence packages retain their own stated authority and non-claim boundaries.
